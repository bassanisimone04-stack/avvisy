The most loved dining scenes of Italian cinema

From Alberto Sordi's macaroni to Roman dolce vita: why food has always been a star of the Italian big screen.

In Italian cinema food is never just food: it is story, identity, comedy and nostalgia. Here are some of the dining scenes that left a mark, in chronological order.

The ritual of Roman cuisine

An American in Rome (1954), directed by Steno, gave us one of the most famous scenes ever, with pasta as the star: Nando Mericoni, after snubbing Italian cuisine in the name of the American dream, finally gives in to macaroni with the line “Maccarone, you provoked me and I’ll destroy you!” — an ironic hymn to the capital’s working-class cuisine.

Fellini’s high society

La Dolce Vita (1960) made Via Veneto a byword for glamour and paparazzi, with the real Café de Paris as the true heart of that era — even though the street scenes were largely rebuilt at Cinecittà.

Conviviality as storytelling

From Welcome to the South, with its dinners among colleagues, to the culinary discovery of Eat Pray Love: the table becomes the place where characters truly meet — the film’s high point is the scene, among the most quoted in recent cinema, of Julia Roberts eating pizza in Naples.

Elegance and nostalgia

The refined dinners of The Great Beauty (2013), Sorrentino’s Oscar winner, and the timeless cafés tell of an Italy suspended between beauty and melancholy.

Food as investigation: Montalbano

Even television has its own ritual at the table: Inspector Montalbano, based on Andrea Camilleri’s novels, never solves a case on an empty stomach. His solitary lunches of fresh fish, often eaten in silence facing the sea at Punta Secca, have become as iconic as the investigations themselves — another way Italian screens turn food into a pause, a thought, a pleasure.

Frequently asked questions

▸ What is the most famous dining scene in Italian cinema?

Alberto Sordi's macaroni in An American in Rome is among the most famous of all time.

▸ Why is food so present in Italian cinema?

It is part of the cultural identity: the table tells stories of family, territory and conviviality.

▸ Which Italian films have memorable dining scenes?

An American in Rome, The Great Beauty, Welcome to the South and the Inspector Montalbano series.

▸ What's the most famous food line in Italian film history?

"Maccarone, you provoked me and I'll destroy you!", Alberto Sordi's line in An American in Rome (1954), directed by Steno — probably the most quoted dining scene in Italian cinema.
