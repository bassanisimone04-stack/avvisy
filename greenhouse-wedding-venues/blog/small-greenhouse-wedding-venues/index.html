Small greenhouse wedding venues: what to ask and where to look

A room built for 200 does not become intimate just because fewer people show up. Here is what actually makes a small greenhouse wedding work, and how to find one.

By GreenhouseVenues editorial

Published 2026-09-06 · Updated 2026-09-06

In short

  • “Small” is about the room, not just the guest count — a propagation house built for 40 beats a ballroom half-filled.
  • Municipal and university glasshouses release small rooms and weekday dates more often than private estates do.
  • Ask about a minimum guest count as well as a maximum: some venues will not book below a certain number.
  • A side room inside a larger conservatory is often cheaper than the same conservatory’s main hall.

What “small” actually means here

There is no industry-standard cutoff, so this directory uses under 50 guests as the useful line for a small or micro-wedding — small enough that the room itself becomes part of the decision, not just the price. A glasshouse built to seat 200 can feel like a warehouse at 40 people. A propagation house or fern room built for 30 to 60 reads as generous at that size, because it was designed for it.

That distinction matters more for glasshouses than for most venue types. Planting beds, potting benches and structural piers take up floor area that a plain hall would not lose, so a room’s character at 40 guests depends heavily on whether 40 was ever the number it was built around.

Where small greenhouse wedding venues actually are

Three patterns show up repeatedly:

  • Municipal and university glasshouses. Built for teaching and propagation rather than events, they are usually smaller than a private estate conservatory, and they release weekday dates more readily because the calendar is not driven by wedding demand alone.
  • The side room inside a larger garden. Many botanical gardens with a grand main conservatory also have a smaller attached house — an orchid room, a fern house, a working greenhouse — that is not always on the main wedding page. It is worth asking about directly.
  • An off-peak booking of a normally-large room. A hall built for 200 is not architecturally small, but a weekday morning micro-wedding there can be priced and run like one, with a simplified setup and a fraction of the usual minimum spend.

Do small venues actually cost less?

Usually, but not because of the guest count alone. Two things do the real work: who owns the building, and which day you book. A municipal or university glasshouse charges a hire rate rather than a market price, and a weekday or off-season date is discounted almost everywhere — both apply more easily to a small wedding than a large one, since a 40-guest booking is easier to fit into a quiet Tuesday than a 200-guest one is. The building itself being small is a smaller factor than either. Our cost guide covers how to structure that comparison venue by venue.

What to ask that a large-wedding checklist will not cover

  1. Is there a minimum guest count, not just a maximum? Some private venues will not book under a set number regardless of how the room reads at that size, because their pricing model assumes a minimum spend per head.
  2. Is the small room a stand-alone booking? A side room inside a larger conservatory may come with shared access, shared restrooms, or public visitors passing through during the day — ask what exclusivity actually means for that specific space.
  3. What does a weekday or off-season date actually save? Ask for the number, not a general assurance of “a discount” — venues that are used to micro-weddings usually have a specific weekday rate already worked out.
  4. Is there an elopement or micro-wedding package that is not on the main wedding page? Several gardens keep one separate from their full-buyout wedding offering, priced and staffed differently.

Quick answers

Small greenhouse wedding venue FAQs

What counts as a small greenhouse wedding venue?

There is no official line, but the directory treats under 50 guests as the useful threshold — small enough that a propagation house or a fern room reads as generous rather than empty, and small enough that many venues will release a weekday date at a fraction of their weekend rate. Use the under-50 capacity filter once a listing’s confirmed capacity is on the page.

How many guests fit in a small greenhouse wedding venue?

It depends entirely on the specific room, and glasshouses vary more than most venue types — planting beds and structural piers eat floor area that a plain hall would not lose. Ask every venue for the seated capacity with a dance floor and the planting where it will actually sit on the day, not the number on its rental brochure.

Are small greenhouse wedding venues cheaper?

Often, but not automatically — a small room inside a grand private estate can cost more than a large hall at a municipal garden. What reliably lowers the number is the venue type (municipal and university glasshouses release smaller rooms more often than private ones do) and the day (a weekday micro-wedding is usually the cheapest date in any venue’s calendar). See the cost guide for how to structure the comparison.

Can I have a micro-wedding at a botanical garden?

Often yes, and it is frequently the easiest way in: a botanical garden that only takes large, full-buyout weddings on weekends may still release a side room or a weekday morning for a small ceremony at a much lower rate. Ask specifically about micro-wedding or elopement packages — some gardens have one that is not advertised alongside their main wedding page.

What should I ask about a small greenhouse wedding venue?

Whether there is a minimum guest count as well as a maximum (some private venues will not book under a certain number regardless of the room’s size); whether the small room is a stand-alone booking or a section of a larger space with shared access; and whether a weekday or off-season date unlocks a materially different rate. All three change the real cost more than the headline capacity does.
