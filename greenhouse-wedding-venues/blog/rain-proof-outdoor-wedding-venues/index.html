Rain-proof outdoor wedding venues: the garden without the weather decision

Most 'wet weather options' are a decision waiting to be made. Here is what genuinely rain-proof looks like, and the questions that tell the two apart.

By Glasshouse Weddings editorial

Published 2026-09-06 · Updated 2026-09-06

  2. Cover the whole chain
  3. The three ways to be dry
  4. What to ask the venue
  5. Finding one
Browse venues

In short

  • “Rain-proof” means no decision has to be made on the day — not that a plan B exists.
  • Cover the whole chain: arrival, ceremony, drinks, dinner, dancing, smokers, vendors.
  • A glazed room keeps the daylight and the planting that made you want to be outdoors.
  • Ask whether moving the ceremony indoors costs anything, and how long the re-set takes.

What “rain-proof” should mean

Most venues marketed as having a wet-weather option are not rain-proof; they are reversible. There is an outdoor plan and an indoor plan, and someone — usually you, usually at about eleven in the morning — has to choose between them while looking at a forecast.

A rain-proof venue removes the decision. Every part of the day already happens under a solid roof, so rain becomes weather rather than a problem. That is the practical case for glasshouses, conservatories and orangeries: they give you the daylight and the planting that made you want to be outside, without the contingency that being outside requires.

Cover the whole chain, not just the dinner

Rain finds the gaps between spaces. When you walk a venue, walk the whole guest journey and ask what happens to each link in it if it is raining hard:

  • Arrival. Where do guests get out of cars, and how far is that from cover?
  • Ceremony. Indoors by default, or moved indoors by decision?
  • Drinks. Often the weakest link — the space between ceremony and dinner is frequently the one outdoors.
  • Dinner and dancing. Usually covered; check whether a re-set is needed between them.
  • Photographs. A glazed room is unusually good here, because the light stays even when the sky does not.
  • Vendors. Caterers and bands loading in across wet grass is how timelines slip.

The three ways to be dry

A glazed venue

A conservatory, palm house, orangery or working greenhouse where the whole day sits under glass. You keep daylight and planting, and the roof, floor, power and restrooms are already there. The trade-off is climate: glass gains heat quickly, so ventilation matters more here than anywhere else.

A marquee

Flexible and available anywhere, but it is a build, not a venue. Flooring, lighting, heating or cooling, power, restrooms and guest arrival all have to be solved and paid for separately — which is why the honest cost comparison is against the whole build, as set out in our cost guide.

An indoor venue with a view

Entirely dry and usually the cheapest to run. What it does not give you is the thing that drew you outdoors in the first place: overhead daylight, and being among planting rather than looking at it.

What to ask the venue

  1. Can the ceremony, dinner and dancing all happen under solid cover? If not, which part cannot?
  2. If the ceremony moves indoors, does it cost anything? Some venues charge for the re-set; some set both from the morning.
  3. By when must the call be made? If there is a decision, there is a deadline — know it before the day.
  4. Is the glazed room heated, and is heat included? Rain in April is a different problem from rain in July.
  5. How is the room ventilated? A sealed glass box in summer solves rain and creates heat.
  6. Where do guests arrive under cover? The car-to-door walk is the detail most often missed.

Quick answers

Rain-proof venue FAQs

What are rain-proof outdoor wedding venues?

Venues that give you the feeling of an outdoor wedding — daylight, planting, a view — with a solid roof over the whole day. Glasshouses, conservatories and orangeries are the clearest example: nothing has to move if it rains, and there is no weather call to make.

Is a marquee a rain-proof outdoor wedding venue?

Partly. A marquee keeps rain off, but it also needs flooring for wet ground, heating or cooling, lighting, power and often a separate plan for guest arrival. A glazed building arrives with all of that already solved, which is why the comparison rarely favours the tent once you price the whole build.

Are greenhouse wedding venues warm enough in bad weather?

In a heated room, yes. Orangeries and modern double-glazed rooms hold heat well; single-glazed Victorian glasshouses are much harder to warm and may be seasonal. Ask what the heating is, what temperature it guarantees, and whether it is included in the hire fee.

Do we still need a wet-weather plan at a conservatory wedding venue?

If every part of the day happens under the glass, no — that is the point of the category. If the ceremony is outdoors with the reception inside, you still need a decision point and a time by which to make it. Ask the venue whether moving the ceremony indoors costs anything or requires a re-set.
