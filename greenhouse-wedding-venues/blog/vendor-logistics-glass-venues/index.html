Vendor logistics at glass venues: what caterers, florists and bands need to know

Glasshouses are rarely built with vendor logistics in mind. Here is the briefing worth sending every vendor before the day.

In short

  • Narrow doors and planting-lined paths can make a standard load-in route unworkable — confirm access, not just capacity.

Why glass venues need a different vendor briefing

A glasshouse is rarely built with catering trucks and stage rigs in mind, and it is often shared with public visitors until closing. Share the venue’s specific logistics with every vendor in advance, rather than assuming standard venue conventions apply — it saves a difficult conversation on the day.

Caterers: power, kitchen access, load-in route

Confirm the electrical supply available on site, whether there is a working prep kitchen or just a serving space, and the actual load-in route — glasshouses often have narrow doors or paths through planting that a standard truck route does not anticipate. Ask the venue directly, and pass the answer to your caterer before the day, not on it.

Florists: what can and cannot touch the glass

Ask whether real flame, loose petals, or hanging installations are allowed near the glazing and structure, and what the venue’s policy is on attaching anything to glass or historic ironwork. Some older glasshouses restrict adhesives or clips that could mark original frames — a restriction a florist used to standard ballrooms may not expect.

Music, sound and curfews

Ask about the venue’s amplified-sound policy and any curfew: glasshouses inside public gardens are frequently close to residential streets and enforce an earlier cutoff than a standalone event venue would. Glass and hard floors also carry sound differently than a carpeted ballroom — a band unfamiliar with the room may want to hear that directly from the venue rather than the couple.

A short logistics briefing to send every vendor

  1. Load-in and load-out windows, and the actual route to the room.
  2. Electrical supply and any restrictions on generators or extension runs.
  3. What can and cannot be attached to glass, ironwork or planting.
  4. Amplified-sound policy and curfew.

Quick answers

Vendor logistics FAQs

What should caterers know before working a glasshouse wedding?

The electrical supply available on site, whether there is a prep kitchen or just a serving space, and the load-in route — glasshouses often have narrow doors or paths through planting that a standard catering truck route does not anticipate. Confirm all three with the venue, not just the couple.

Do florists need to know anything specific about glass venues?

Whether real flame, loose petals or hanging installations are allowed near the glazing and structure, and what the venue’s policy is on attaching anything to the glass or ironwork. Some historic glasshouses restrict adhesives or clips that could mark original frames.

Is load-in harder at a botanical garden than a standard venue?

Often, because vendors are sharing paths with public visitors until the garden closes, and load-in windows are frequently fixed rather than flexible. Share the venue’s specific load-in rules with every vendor in advance rather than assuming standard venue logistics apply.

What about sound and bands at a glasshouse?

Ask about the venue’s amplified-sound policy and any curfew, since glasshouses in public gardens are often close to residential areas and enforce an earlier cutoff than a standalone event venue would. Glass and hard floors also carry sound differently than a carpeted ballroom — a band that has not played the room before may want to hear that from the venue directly.

Do caterers and other vendors need their own liability insurance at a glass venue?

Usually yes, and public gardens and estate conservatories tend to enforce it more consistently than a standard rented hall — commonly a certificate of insurance naming the venue as additional insured. Confirm the required coverage amount and the deadline for providing proof with every vendor, not just the caterer, since some venues expect it from florists and band or DJ crews too.
