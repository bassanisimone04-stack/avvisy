Elopements and micro-ceremonies in a glasshouse

A room built small reads as generous for a handful of guests. Here is how to find and book one for an elopement or micro-ceremony.

By GreenhouseVenues editorial

Published 2026-09-18 · Updated 2026-09-18

In short

  • A room built small reads as generous for an elopement; a room built large can feel empty even at a discount.
  • Many gardens keep a separate elopement or micro-ceremony package, not always advertised with the main wedding page.
  • Some public gardens allow a small ceremony within general admission hours for a modest fee — worth asking about directly.
  • Confirm exactly what space, time and privacy a small package actually includes.

Why glass suits an elopement particularly well

A room designed for 200 can feel like a warehouse with ten people in it. A propagation house, fern room or side conservatory built with a small footprint in mind reads as generous, not empty, at the same guest count — because it was designed for it. See our small greenhouse wedding venues guide for more on finding rooms built at this scale.

Ask about a separate elopement package

Many gardens keep a distinct elopement or micro-ceremony package — priced and staffed differently from the full wedding offering, for roughly 2 to 20 guests — that is not always advertised alongside the main wedding page. Ask directly rather than assuming the full wedding package, with its higher minimums, is the only option.

Marrying within general admission hours

A few gardens allow a very small ceremony within their normal public opening hours for a modest fee, rather than a full private-event rental. This is worth asking about specifically if your guest count is very small, since it can be substantially cheaper than a standard wedding rental — the trade-off is that other visitors will be present.

What a small package should actually include

At minimum, confirm exactly which space you get access to and for how long, whether other visitors will be present during your ceremony, and whether photography time is included or billed separately. Small packages sometimes leave these details unstated where a full wedding contract would spell them out — ask rather than assume.

Questions to ask before booking a small ceremony

  1. Is there a dedicated elopement or micro-ceremony package, separate from the full wedding offering?
  2. Is a small ceremony available within general admission hours, at a lower fee?
  3. Exactly which space and how much time does the package include?
  4. Will other visitors be present, and is photography time included?

Quick answers

Elopement & micro-ceremony FAQs

Are glasshouses a good fit for elopements and micro-weddings?

Often yes — a room built for 40 rather than 200 can feel generous instead of empty, and many gardens release smaller rooms or weekday dates specifically for micro-weddings at a lower rate than their full-buyout wedding package.

Do venues have a separate elopement package?

Many do, and it is not always advertised alongside the main wedding page — some gardens keep a distinct elopement or micro-ceremony package, priced and staffed differently, for 2 to 20 guests. Ask directly rather than assuming the full wedding package is the only option.

Can we elope at a botanical garden without a big rental?

Sometimes — a few gardens allow a small ceremony within general admission hours for a modest fee, rather than a full private-event rental. This is worth asking about specifically if your guest count is very small, since it can be far cheaper than a standard wedding rental.

What should an elopement package include at a glasshouse?

At minimum: confirmation of exactly which space you get access to and for how long, whether other visitors will be present, and whether photography time is included or billed separately. Small packages sometimes leave these unstated where a full wedding contract would spell them out.
