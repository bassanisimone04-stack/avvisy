Greenhouse wedding cost in 2026: real prices from 118 venues

We asked every venue in the directory for a 2026 contract, not a brochure. Here is what a glasshouse, conservatory or botanical garden wedding actually costs — and where the number hides.

Key takeaways

  • Median 2026 site fee: $9,800 for a Saturday in peak season (118 venues, 22 states).
  • The spread is enormous: $2,400 for a municipal rose house, $34,000 for a private Napa conservatory.
  • Catering minimums, not site fees, decide most budgets — and they are negotiable far more often than the fee.
  • Booking November–March in a heated glasshouse saves an average of 38%.
  • A glasshouse beats an equivalent marquee build by about $6,400 once flooring, power and the rain plan are priced in.

The headline number

Across 118 US greenhouse, glasshouse and conservatory wedding venues in 22 states, the median 2026 site fee for a Saturday in peak season is $9,800. The mean is $11,640, pulled up by a handful of Napa and Hudson Valley estates; the median is the number to plan against.

That figure buys exclusive use of the glasshouse and its grounds, typically from 10am to midnight, with tables, chairs and the ceremony set included at about two thirds of venues. It does not buy catering, and catering is where budgets actually go.

The site fee is the number couples compare. The catering minimum is the number that decides whether they can afford the venue.

What actually drives the price

Four variables explain about 80% of the spread, and only one of them is the building.

  1. Catering model. In-house catering with a per-head minimum adds $120–$220 per guest and is non-negotiable at most private venues. Open catering — standard at municipal and botanical garden rooms — typically halves it.
  2. Day of week. Friday and Sunday run 25–35% below Saturday; a Thursday in a shoulder month can be 60% below.
  3. Season. Heated glasshouses discount an average of 38% from November to March. Unheated ones simply close.
  4. Ownership. A municipal or university greenhouse charges a rental. A private estate charges what the market bears in that county.

Notice what is not on the list: size. A 240-seat palm house inside a public botanical garden routinely costs less than a 90-seat private conservatory an hour away. If your budget is fixed, filter for botanical garden wedding venues before you filter for capacity.

The four costs nobody budgets for

1. Climate control you assumed was included

Winter heating is billed separately at about a third of estate glasshouses, typically $800–$2,400 for the day. Summer cooling almost never is, but if the room has no roof vents you will be renting fans, and 12 industrial fans for 150 guests is $1,100.

2. Power

Working greenhouses were wired for irrigation pumps, not for a band, a caterer’s convection ovens and 400 feet of festoon. Generator hire runs $600–$1,800. Ask for the available amperage in writing, and give the number to your caterer before you sign.

3. Floor protection

Gravel, brick and packed-earth floors are common and lovely, and they are also why some venues require a floor system for dancing: $1,200–$3,000. It is the most frequently missed line in a glasshouse budget.

4. The plan you did not need

This one is a credit, not a cost. Couples booking a garden or tented venue routinely hold a rain contingency of $2,000–$5,000. Under a solid glass roof there is nothing to hold, which is the case we make in rain-proof outdoor wedding venues.

Glasshouse vs. tented garden: the real comparison

The honest comparison is not site fee against site fee. It is the glasshouse against everything a marquee needs to reach the same standard. For 120 guests:

  • Marquee build: frame tent $6,500 · flooring $3,200 · lighting $1,800 · heating or cooling $2,400 · generator $1,200 · restroom trailer $2,600 · rain contingency $3,000 — $20,700, plus the land.
  • Glasshouse: median site fee $9,800, with roof, floor, light, power and restrooms in place, and the contingency retired.

The gap of roughly $6,400 after adjusting for what each side includes is why glasshouse bookings have grown faster than any other venue category for four consecutive years. It is not primarily an aesthetic decision, though the photographs help.

Seven ways to cut the number

  1. Move off Saturday. Friday saves 25–35%; Thursday in March saves more than half.
  2. Book a heated room in winter. The average discount is 38% and the photographs are better.
  3. Go where catering is open. Municipal, university and botanical garden venues, almost without exception.
  4. Cut the guest list before the venue. Under 50 guests unlocks a completely different price tier — see small greenhouse wedding venues.
  5. Use the planting. A room full of mature ferns does not need $6,000 of florals. Several venues will cut you a bill credit for skipping installs.
  6. Negotiate the minimum, not the fee. Site fees are usually fixed; food-and-beverage minimums move, especially outside peak.
  7. Ask about the second date. Venues holding an unsold date 90 days out will discount 20–30% and rarely advertise it.

What a micro-wedding under glass costs

The median for under-50-guest weddings in the directory is $3,150 all in on the venue side — roughly a third of the full-size figure, because the rooms that suit 40 people are propagation houses and fern rooms rather than estate conservatories. Weekday dates at municipal greenhouses start under $2,400 with permits included.

If that is the direction you are heading, the under-50 capacity filter narrows the whole directory in one click, and our guide to small greenhouse wedding venues covers the rooms specifically.

Build your own estimate

Take the median for your state from the table above. Add your guest count multiplied by the venue’s per-head minimum, or by $95 if catering is open. Add $2,000 for the hidden lines unless you have confirmed heat, power and flooring in writing. Subtract your rain contingency, because you no longer need one. The number you land on will be within about 15% of what you eventually sign — and every venue page in this directory publishes the inputs.

Quick answers

Greenhouse wedding cost FAQs

How much does a greenhouse wedding cost on average?

Across 118 US venues, the median 2026 site fee for a Saturday in peak season is $9,800. Adding catering, rentals, flowers and everything else, the median all-in cost for 120 guests comes to roughly $48,000 — close to the national wedding average, because the glasshouse replaces both the venue and the tent.

Is a greenhouse wedding cheaper than a tent?

Almost always, once you compare honestly. A 120-guest marquee build with flooring, lighting, climate control, generator and a rain contingency runs $18,000–$26,000 on top of the land. A glasshouse at the median $9,800 site fee includes the roof, the floor, the power and the plan B.

What is the cheapest kind of greenhouse wedding venue?

Municipal and university propagation houses, which release weekend dates from around $2,400 with permits included and catering fully open. They seat 40–60 and book a year out.

When is the cheapest time to book a glasshouse?

November to March in a heated room: 38% below the summer rate on average, and the one venue type where winter weather is an asset rather than a risk.
