The kinds of greenhouse wedding venue you will find in Minnesota

“Greenhouse” covers several quite different buildings, and the difference shapes your whole day. A working greenhouse is a growing structure, built for plants rather than people: usually smaller, usually the cheapest way to marry under a transparent roof, and the most likely to need power, heating or floor protection brought in. A conservatory is a glazed room attached to a building. An orangery is its masonry relative — solid piers between tall glazed openings, and far easier to heat. A palm house is the tall domed type found in public gardens, and an atrium its modern equivalent.

Botanical garden venues in Minnesota may be any of these, wrapped in a collection that usually also gives you a ceremony lawn. They tend to offer the largest glazed rooms available locally, in exchange for approved-caterer lists, fixed load-in windows and public opening hours.

What to ask a Minnesota venue before you book

Glass buildings differ from ordinary venues in four specific ways. These questions separate a room that works in your month from one that only photographs well:

  • Seated capacity with a dance floor, with the planting where it will be on the day. Glazed rooms lose floor area to beds and structural piers.
  • Ventilation, and what it holds. Opening roof vents, side louvers, shade cloth or mechanical cooling — and the temperature the venue will stand behind on a hot afternoon. A glass roof gains heat fast; ventilation is what makes the difference, not the local climate.
  • Heating, and who pays for it. Included in the hire fee or billed separately, and what is guaranteed in the coldest month you would consider.
  • Catering policy and minimum spend. Usually a bigger lever on the total than the hire fee itself — see the cost guide.

Frequently asked

How much does a greenhouse wedding venue in Minnesota cost?

Rates in Minnesota vary by an order of magnitude between a municipal or university glasshouse and a private estate conservatory, and most venues quote a hire fee separately from catering. Rather than repeat a number that would be out of date by the time you read it, each listing links to the venue’s own page — and our cost guide lists every line item to ask about.

Which greenhouse wedding venues in Minnesota allow outside catering?

Public gardens and university glasshouses in Minnesota usually work from an approved-caterer list; private venues more often require in-house catering with a minimum spend. Both policies are normally stated on the venue’s own events page, which every listing links to. Confirm it early: it moves a budget more than the hire fee does.

Are there small greenhouse wedding venues in Minnesota for under 50 guests?

Smaller rooms exist within most large glasshouses — propagation houses, fern rooms and side wings — and many venues in Minnesota release them for weekday micro-weddings. Ask each venue which rooms it offers below 50 guests; confirmed capacities appear on the listing as we verify them.

What are the best botanical garden wedding venues in Minnesota?

The botanical gardens in Minnesota that run wedding programmes are listed above, each linking to its own events page. They generally offer the largest glazed rooms in their city, in exchange for approved-caterer lists, fixed load-in windows and public opening hours.

How hot does a glasshouse get in a Minnesota summer?

It depends on the ventilation rather than the outdoor temperature. Rooms with opening roof vents, side louvers, shade cloth or mechanical cooling stay close to ambient; rooms without them can climb well above it on a sunny afternoon anywhere, Minnesota included. Ask the venue what it holds at 4pm in August, and treat a vague answer as an answer.

When is the best season for a Minnesota greenhouse wedding?

Spring and autumn are the safe answer almost everywhere: enough daylight for the glass to earn its keep, without the solar gain of high summer. Winter works well in a heated room and is usually the cheapest date in the calendar. Ask about heating and ventilation for the specific month you are considering rather than the season in general.
