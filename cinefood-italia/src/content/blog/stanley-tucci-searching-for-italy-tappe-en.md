There is one series that made the world fall in love with Italian cuisine:
**Stanley Tucci: Searching for Italy**, which aired on **CNN** starting
February 14, 2021, for two seasons, winning **3 Emmy Awards** for
Outstanding Hosted Nonfiction Series. The actor, of Calabrian heritage (his
grandfather was from Marzi, near Cosenza, his grandmother from Serra San
Bruno), travels region by region in search of authentic dishes, among
markets, trattorias and fine restaurants. A perfect food itinerary to
retrace.

## The regions of the journey

Tucci explores some of the tastiest destinations in Italy:

- **Naples and Campania**: pizza, ragù, buffalo mozzarella;
- **Rome and Lazio**: cacio e pepe, carbonara, artichokes;
- **Bologna and Emilia-Romagna**: tortellini, tagliatelle al ragù, mortadella;
- **Tuscany**: steak, pici, wine;
- **Sicily**: pasta alla Norma, arancini, cannoli;
- **Milan and Lombardy**: risotto and veal cutlet.

## Why it is a perfect guide

Each episode is essentially a mini-itinerary: it shows where to eat, what to order
and why those dishes tell the story of a territory. It is the ideal way to plan a
food trip in Italy.

## The show's venues, verified

We checked the venues listed by CNN in the series' official articles one
by one, keeping only those still open:

- **[La Reginella](/en/locale/la-reginella-roma)**, Rome — in the Jewish
  Ghetto, Tucci tastes carciofi alla giudia at the height of artichoke season.
- **[La Notizia 53](/en/locale/la-notizia-napoli)**, Naples — Enzo Coccia,
  the first pizza maker listed by Michelin, makes him a classic pizza. Today
  the menu features one named after Tucci.
- **[Lo Scoglio](/en/locale/lo-scoglio-nerano)**, Nerano — on the beach at
  Marina del Cantone, the spaghetti alla Nerano Tucci calls his favourite dish.
- **[Casa Artusi](/en/locale/casa-artusi-forlimpopoli)**, Forlimpopoli —
  the cooking school dedicated to Pellegrino Artusi, with a hand-rolled pasta
  workshop.
