There is a scene in *Eat Pray Love* that made millions of viewers hungry: Liz,
played by **Julia Roberts**, biting into a margherita pizza in Naples and finally
surrendering to the pleasure of food. That place really exists, and it is one of
the temples of Neapolitan pizza:
**[L'Antica Pizzeria da Michele](/en/locale/pizzeria-da-michele-napoli)**.

## Where the pizza scene was filmed

We are in the **Forcella** district, on Via Cesare Sersale. Da Michele has been
open since **1870** and has made simplicity its philosophy: the historic menu
has only two pizzas, **marinara** and **margherita**, baked in a wood-fired oven
according to tradition. That very margherita is the star of the film's scene.

## What to expect (and what to order)

Da Michele is an authentic experience: a bare-bones setting, shared tables and
often a **queue outside** (you take a number and wait). But that is part of the
ritual. Order the **margherita**, perhaps with "double mozzarella", and a beer or
a soda. Don't look for frills: here it is all about the dough, the tomato and the
mozzarella.

> Tip: go at off-peak times (early lunch or mid-afternoon) to cut the wait.

## A Naples itinerary in the footsteps of the film

After the pizza, Forcella and Naples' historic centre are worth a stroll:
Spaccanapoli, San Gregorio Armeno, the seafront. You will find the address and
map on the **[Pizzeria da Michele](/en/locale/pizzeria-da-michele-napoli)** page
and the film's other locations on
**[Eat Pray Love](/en/film/mangia-prega-ama)**.

## In short

If you loved the pizza scene in *Eat Pray Love*, sitting down (after the queue)
at **[Michele](/en/locale/pizzeria-da-michele-napoli)** is a must: it is the most
authentic way to taste the Naples of the film, one margherita at a time.
