There's a dish that made a small Trastevere trattoria famous worldwide:
the tagliolini cacio e pepe at **Ristorante Roma Sparita**, served in an
edible parmesan basket instead of a bowl. It was made famous by
**Anthony Bourdain**, in the Rome episode of his series *No
Reservations*.

## "Restaurant X": the name kept secret

In the episode, which aired on Travel Channel in 2010, Bourdain tasted
the restaurant's cacio e pepe and was so won over that he initially kept
its name secret, introducing it only as "Restaurant X" — joking that he
didn't want to "kill" a place he loved too much by drawing in crowds of
tourists. The name didn't stay secret for long, though: **Ristorante
Roma Sparita**, on Piazza di Santa Cecilia.

## A family trattoria, not a set

Unlike many locations on this site, **[Roma
Sparita](/en/locale/roma-sparita-trastevere)** isn't a film set but a
real neighborhood trattoria, run by the same family for over thirty
years, in the heart of Trastevere. It's exactly this authenticity —
far from the more touristy circuits when Bourdain discovered it — that
struck the New York chef and writer.
