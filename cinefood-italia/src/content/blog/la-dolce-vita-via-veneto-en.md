No street tells the story of cinema like **Via Vittorio Veneto** in Rome. In the
1960s, with Federico Fellini's *La Dolce Vita*, it became the symbol of glamour,
paparazzi and Italian high society.

## The heart of the "dolce vita"

Between elegant hotels and cafés facing the street, Via Veneto was the stage of
the Rome that never slept. A little-known fact: the scenes set on the street
were not filmed on the real one, but on a faithful reconstruction Fellini
had built on **Studio 5 at Cinecittà**, so he could control every detail of
lighting and extras. The real Via Veneto, though, remains the place that
inspired it all: it's here, in real late-1950s Rome, that the paparazzi
phenomenon the film portrays was actually born.

## The film's most famous scene isn't on Via Veneto

A common mix-up: the film's most iconic scene, **Anita Ekberg** wading into
the **Trevi Fountain** with Marcello Mastroianni, isn't set on Via Veneto at
all but at the Trevi Fountain, in another part of the historic center.
Together, the two locations tell the two souls of Fellini's Rome: the
high-society world of the cafés, and the monumental, scenic Rome of its
fountains.

## Not only Fellini

Rome has always been a film city: from the trattorias of
*[Eat Pray Love](/en/film/mangia-prega-ama)* to the terraces of *[The Great
Beauty](/en/film/la-grande-bellezza)*, to the strolls of *[To Rome with
Love](/en/film/to-rome-with-love)*. Every corner has its own story on the
big screen. If you're curious about the real link between Via Veneto and
the Café de Paris, the venue that inspired Fellini, also read our deep dive
on **[La Dolce Vita and the Café de Paris](/en/blog/la-dolce-vita-via-veneto-cafe-de-paris)**.

## What to see today along the street

Walking Via Veneto today you'll still find historic hotels like the
**Excelsior**, the famous **Harry's Bar** (still open), and the climb up to
**Porta Pinciana** leading into the gardens of Villa Borghese — a route
that combines cinema, architecture and one of the most elegant corners of
the capital.
