No film has made Sicily more famous than *The Godfather*. And yet the town
that gives the Corleone family its name, **Corleone** itself, never appears
on screen: Francis Ford Coppola filmed elsewhere, in two villages near
Messina that had stayed faithful to the 1920s Sicily described in Mario
Puzo's novel.

## Why Coppola didn't film in Corleone

By the 1970s, the town of Corleone had already become too modern to convey
the novel's atmosphere. The production instead looked for smaller,
less-altered villages, and found them in the hills above Taormina:
**Savoca** and **Forza d'Agrò**, just a few kilometres apart.

## Savoca: the heart of the shoot

**[Savoca](/en/savoca)** is the village where most of the Sicilian scenes
are set. It's here that Michael Corleone, exiled in Sicily after the
attempt on his father's life, falls in love with Apollonia. The most
visited location is **[Bar Vitelli](/en/locale/bar-vitelli-savoca)**, on
Piazza Fossia: the bar where Michael formally asks Apollonia's father for
her hand, inside the 18th-century Palazzo Trimarchi. The venue didn't even
have an official name before the film — today it looks almost identical to
1971, with the same panoramic terrace over the valley.

## Forza d'Agrò: the wedding

A few minutes from Savoca, **Forza d'Agrò** provided the backdrop for
Michael and Apollonia's wedding scene, shot on the church forecourt. It's a
medieval hilltop village with narrow lanes and, on clear days, views
stretching from the Ionian coast to Mount Etna.

## An itinerary through both villages

Savoca and Forza d'Agrò are about ten minutes apart by car, and can both be
visited in the same afternoon starting from Taormina, roughly 30 minutes
further south. In Savoca, besides Bar Vitelli, the Chiesa Madre and the
belvedere over the Agrò valley are worth a stop; in Forza d'Agrò, the old
town and the remains of the Norman castle.

> Practical tip: Bar Vitelli is open year-round, but in the summer months
> it can get very busy with fans retracing the film. Always check current
> opening hours before setting off.
