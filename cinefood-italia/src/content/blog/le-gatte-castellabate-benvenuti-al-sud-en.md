**In short.** The dinner among colleagues in *Welcome to the South* (*Benvenuti al Sud*) is set at
the **Porto delle Gatte**, in **Santa Maria di Castellabate**, and the
**[Ristorante Le Gatte](/en/locale/ristorante-le-gatte)** stands on the port. We list it as
**linked to the film**: the scene is set in the port, but we found no source confirming the
restaurant's dining room as a set.

- **Address:** Via Gennaro Naso 22, Santa Maria di Castellabate (SA).
- **The film's post office:** it doesn't exist, it is a set on Castellabate's square.
- **Before you go:** check that it's open: online information is mixed.

---

After the success of *Welcome to the South* (2010, directed by Luca Miniero, a remake of the French
*Bienvenue chez les Ch'tis*), many visitors came to Castellabate looking for the dinner
restaurant. The answer is more nuanced than many guides suggest: here is what we know and what we don't.

## The welcome dinner at the Porto delle Gatte

In the film the new post-office manager, played by **Claudio Bisio**, is welcomed by his
colleagues with a lavish dinner. According to the sources we found, the scene is set in the area of
the **Porto delle Gatte**, in the hamlet of **Santa Maria di Castellabate**. A travel guide describes the
port as an old landing place from the 12th century, with an arched structure, and explains the name by
the effect of the lit arcades, which from afar look like cats' eyes. We report these details as such,
without being able to check them against historical sources.

## Ristorante Le Gatte: what is true and what isn't

On the port stands **[Ristorante Le Gatte](/en/locale/ristorante-le-gatte)**, at Via Gennaro Naso 22.
Its name matches the port's, and guides cite it as the place where "the dinner was filmed". Looking for
confirmation we found the scene placed in the port, but **no source saying it was shot in the restaurant's
dining room**. That's why we moved Le Gatte from the "real set" category to **venue linked to the film**: it
exists and is where the scene is set, but we can't guarantee it was used for filming.

Online opinions are mixed: some praise the food, the portions and the view, others criticise slow service.
One online listing also marked it as temporarily closed. **Check hours and availability before you go.**

## The post office that isn't there

Castellabate's little square, **Piazza 10 Ottobre 1123**, is the heart of the film, but the post office
doesn't exist: it is a set. According to a travel article, a bar on the square was redesigned to create the
facade. We don't know which one, so we don't list **[Bar La Piazzetta](/en/locale/bar-la-piazzetta-castellabate)**
as a location, only as a bar on the film's square.

## Other places in the film

One guide reports that other scenes were shot outside Castellabate, such as around Torretta and at the
Persano military base, that the railway station is invented, and that some sequences set in the North were
shot in Lazio. We could not confirm this elsewhere.

## Planning your visit

Castellabate is split between the **old village** on the hill, **Santa Maria** by the sea and **San Marco**
on the little harbour. To follow the film: the village square, the belvedere with the view over the gulf and
the port of Santa Maria. All the venues in the area are on the **[Castellabate](/en/castellabate)** page, the film
on the **[Welcome to the South](/en/film/benvenuti-al-sud)** page and the full itinerary in
**[Welcome to the South: Filming Locations in Castellabate](/en/blog/benvenuti-al-sud-castellabate)**.

## In short

If you're looking for "the Welcome to the South restaurant", the honest answer is: a venue on the port where
the scene is set, but not confirmed as a set. It's worth the visit for the place and the Cilento cooking, not for
finding the film's exact dining room.
