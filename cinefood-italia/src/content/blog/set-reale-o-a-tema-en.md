When you look for "a film's restaurant" you can run into two different
things. Understanding the difference helps you choose the right
experience — which is why every card on our site cites the source used to
verify it.

## Real set

It's a venue where the film was **actually shot**, according to the sources
cited on the card. Here the magic is authentic: you sit where the characters
sat. On our cards you recognise it by the **Real set** badge.

## Film-related: real, but not filmed there

A venue can be tied to a film without being a location. Some are the real
place of the phenomenon portrayed: the **[Café de
Paris](/en/locale/cafe-de-paris-roma)** on Via Veneto (now closed), the heart of
Rome's "dolce vita", while the street scenes were shot on a set at
Cinecittà. Others are simply places to eat near a set, like Duomocafè in
Crema. In these cases the badge is **Film-related** and we always explain it
clearly on the card and in the articles. Compare with a **real set** like
**[Bar Vitelli](/en/locale/bar-vitelli-savoca)** in Savoca, which appears
literally on screen.

## Cinema-themed

It's a venue **inspired** by cinema — décor, posters, themed menu — but **not a
real location**. These are fun, scenic spots, perfect for enthusiasts, but don't
expect to find a scene from the film there. We mark them with the **
Cinema-themed** badge (currently no venue on the site is in this category).

## How to tell them apart on CineFood Italia

On the [All venues](/en/locali) page you can **filter by category** and choose
only the real sets, only the film-related venues or only the themed ones. Each card shows the matching badge,
so you always know what to expect.

> A tip: always check the sources. Film appearances can be told in different ways,
> and the data must be confirmed. On our site, we always cite the sources
> used to verify each location.

Explore the [directory](/en/locali) and filter by the category you prefer.
