There's a table, inside one of Italy's oldest cafés, that visitors to
Naples always ask to sit at. It's not just any table: it belongs to
**Commissioner Ricciardi**, the lead of the Rai 1 series based on
Maurizio De Giovanni's novels.

## 1930s Naples, without artificial sets

*Commissioner Ricciardi* tells the story of a Naples caught between
fascism and high society, through the eyes of an investigator who can
perceive the last thoughts of murder victims. The production chose to
recreate that era using real places in the city rather than built sets:
according to the **Campania Region Film Commission**, the official
locations include the Teatro San Carlo, the Teatro Sannazaro,
Capodimonte, Villa Pignatelli, the Royal Palace of Portici — and the
Gran Caffè Gambrinus.

## The Gambrinus, transformed into the 1930s

Founded in **1860**, **[Gran Caffè
Gambrinus](/en/locale/gran-caffe-gambrinus-napoli)** is one of Italy's
most celebrated historic cafés, facing Piazza del Plebiscito and Piazza
Trieste e Trento — a literary salon frequented for over a century by
intellectuals and artists. For filming, the venue was transformed into
the period café the commissioner visits: scenes are set right at his
favourite table, overlooking Piazza del Plebiscito.

## The table reserved forever

After the show's success, as the venue itself explains on its official
website, Gambrinus's owners — the Rosati brothers — decided to
permanently reserve that table: today it carries a dedicated plaque,
with a trench coat identical to the one worn by the lead on set
displayed alongside it.
