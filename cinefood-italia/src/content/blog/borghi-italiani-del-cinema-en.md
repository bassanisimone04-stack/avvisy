Cinema has made many small Italian villages immortal. Visiting them means
combining two pleasures: walking among the film locations and sitting down to
taste the flavours of the territory. Here are eight stops not to miss, from
south to north.

## South and islands

- **Savoca (Sicily)** — the alleys of *[The Godfather](/en/film/il-padrino)*
  and the granita at **[Bar Vitelli](/en/locale/bar-vitelli-savoca)**, the
  village bar that has barely changed since 1971.
- **Palazzo Adriano (Sicily)** — the scenic Piazza Umberto I, heart of
  *[Cinema Paradiso](/en/film/nuovo-cinema-paradiso)*, now also home to a
  museum dedicated to the film.
- **Punta Secca (Sicily)** — the house and seafood flavours of
  *[Montalbano](/en/film/il-commissario-montalbano)*, between the seafront
  and fish-based tables.
- **Salina (Aeolian Islands)** — the atmosphere of
  *[Il Postino](/en/film/il-postino)* among prized capers and sweet malvasia
  wine, on the island where Massimo Troisi shot his final film.
- **Castellabate (Cilento)** — the comedy of
  *[Welcome to the South](/en/film/benvenuti-al-sud)* and fresh fish on the
  port of Santa Maria di Castellabate.

## Centre and North

- **Rome** — a chapter of its own, from *[The Great Beauty](/en/film/la-grande-bellezza)*
  to *[Eat Pray Love](/en/film/mangia-prega-ama)*, with more food locations
  than any other city on our site.
- **Crema (Lombardy)** — the suspended summer of
  *[Call Me by Your Name](/en/film/chiamami-col-tuo-nome)*, between Piazza
  del Duomo and tortelli cremaschi.
- **Venice** — the historic cafés of
  *[The Talented Mr. Ripley](/en/film/il-talento-di-mr-ripley)*, starting
  with Caffè Florian, the oldest in Italy.
