There's a scene every *Màkari* fan recognizes instantly: Marilù's
restaurant, with its terrace overlooking the sea, where Saverio Lamanna
and Peppe Piccionello often meet up between cases. That breathtaking
view isn't invented — it's the **Tonnara di Scopello**, one of Sicily's
most photographed spots.

## A set inside a real monument

According to the official **Italy for Movies** portal, the restaurant
of Marilù's character (Antonella Attili) was built inside the old
Scopello tuna fishery, with its sea stacks in the background. Today the
tonnara no longer fishes: it's a historic site open to the public, with
a café and beach area, one of the most loved spots on the coast between
Castellammare del Golfo and San Vito Lo Capo.

## The real Marilù isn't here

One detail confuses many viewers: the character of Marilù is inspired
by a real person, **Marilù Terrasi**, a source of inspiration for
writer Gaetano Savatteri. In real life, though, Marilù runs a different
restaurant — **Il Pocho**, near Isulidda beach, in the Gulf of Macari —
not to be confused with the Tonnara di Scopello used for filming: two
distinct real places, each with a different role in the show's story.

## Where to find it

The Tonnara di Scopello is located at Largo Tonnara, in the municipality
of Castellammare del Golfo, a few minutes from San Vito Lo Capo and the
Zingaro Nature Reserve — another location loved by the production. It's
open to visitors during the day, with a sea-stack view that's already
the real star of the scene.
