No secret agent loves Italy quite like **James Bond**: as many as eight 007 films
were shot in the country. Between chases and Martinis "shaken, not stirred", the
Italian locations are also an invitation to the table. Here are the three most
iconic and where to eat like a true agent.

## Matera — *No Time to Die*
The Aston Martin chase through the **Sassi of Matera** (Basilicata) is one of the
most spectacular scenes in recent cinema. Here the food is Lucanian: **Matera PGI
bread**, crispy peppers, cavatelli, cured meats and caciocavallo podolico. A
cuisine as ancient as the city's caves.

## Venice — *Casino Royale*
In Venice, Bond shows his most romantic side with Vesper Lynd, between the Grand
Canal and the palaces on the water. For a 007-style aperitivo, **cicchetti** in
the bacari and a spritz are a must. And for a "cinematic" coffee, there's the
historic **[Caffè Florian](/en/locale/caffe-florian)** in St Mark's Square.
Discover the venues of **[Venice](/en/venezia)**.

## Lake Como — *Casino Royale*
On Lake Como, Bond recovers at the splendid **Villa del Balbianello**, in
Lenno, where Vesper Lynd nurses him back to health after Le Chiffre's
torture. It's not just a Bond location: the same villa hosted Anakin and
Padmé's first kiss in *Star Wars: Episode II*, and is considered one of
the most photographed film locations in history. It's open to visitors
from mid-March to November. Lake Como cuisine is refined and lake-based:
**risotto with perch**, missoltini, and the great wines of nearby
Valtellina. An elegant finale, just like the character.

## In short

Retracing 007's Italian itinerary means crossing three different Italies — the
stone South, the lagoon and the lake — each with its own table. A journey
straight out of a film. Browse all the **[films](/en/film)** with their food
locations.
