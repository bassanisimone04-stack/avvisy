"Maccarone, you provoked me and I'll destroy you!" — few lines of Italian
cinema are as famous as **Alberto Sordi**'s in *An American in Rome*
(1954), directed by **Steno**. The scene in which Nando Mericoni, a young
man from Trastevere obsessed with the myth of America, finally gives in to
macaroni after snubbing pasta, has gone down in history. Here is how to
find that working-class Rome and its cuisine.

## The spirit of the film: authentic Rome

*An American in Rome* is an affectionate portrait of post-war working-class
Rome, made of alleys, trattorias and generous dishes — with Nando Mericoni
presented as a young man from **Trastevere**, the district that embodies
the capital's working-class soul. Exterior filming took place at several
real locations, including **Via del Portico d'Ottavia** and **Vicolo
Savini**. The famous macaroni scene itself, though, was shot in a tavern
set built specifically for the film, not a real existing venue: there's no
single "real Nando Mericoni restaurant" to visit, but the neighbourhood
and atmosphere it portrays are genuine.

## The dishes to order

To pay homage to Nando, you obviously start with **macaroni** — or rather, with
Roman pasta in all its forms: **cacio e pepe**, **amatriciana**, **carbonara**,
**rigatoni with pajata**. Don't miss the **Roman-style artichokes** and the oxtail
stew.

## An itinerary in the historic centre

The area between Campo de' Fiori, Piazza Navona and the Ghetto — also
crossed by the film's exterior shots — is today the realm of the genuine
Roman trattoria. A walk on foot, with tasty stops, is the best way to
savour Sordi's city. Discover all the venues of **[Rome](/en/roma)**,
including the city's other real food locations in our
**[Rome film restaurants itinerary](/en/blog/roma-nel-cinema-ristoranti-da-film)**,
and the film's stops on **[An American in Rome](/en/film/un-americano-a-roma)**.

## In short

Reliving *An American in Rome* means strolling between Trastevere and the
Ghetto, sitting down at a genuine Roman trattoria and ordering a plate of
pasta — letting the macaroni "provoke" you as it did Nando Mericoni, maybe
at one of the real venues in our other Rome itineraries.
