Phil Rosenthal, the creator of *Everybody Loves Raymond*, travels the world
for Netflix with one simple rule: eat where the locals eat. In the Venice
episode of *Somebody Feed Phil* — the first of season two — he skips the
tourist restaurants around St Mark's Square and heads into bacari, narrow
calli and historic trattorias. Two of those addresses have become must-visit
stops for anyone who's seen the show.

## Al Covo, "maybe the best restaurant in Venice"

In the Castello district, a few minutes' walk from St Mark's,
**[Al Covo](/en/locale/al-covo-venezia)** is a seafood restaurant run by
Cesare and his wife Diane. Here Phil tastes boiled mantis shrimp and spider
crab, and lets slip the line that made the place famous among fans of the
show: this, he says, might just be the best restaurant in Venice. Lagoon
cooking, the catch of the day, no frills.

## Vini da Arturo, a pork chop in a city of fish

On a street with an ominous name — Calle degli Assassini, in the San Marco
district — **[Vini da Arturo](/en/locale/vini-da-arturo-venezia)** is a small
trattoria open for over fifty years, with a rarity for Venice: meat-focused
cooking. Phil comes for the house signature, the pork chop with vinegar,
served with marinated eggplant. Just a few tables, cash only: book ahead.

## How to plan it

Both places are in the historic centre, within walking distance of St
Mark's Square. Al Covo closes on Wednesdays and Thursdays, Vini da Arturo on
Sundays: keep that in mind if you only have a few days in town. To round off
the day, do as Phil does and spend an afternoon on a bacaro crawl, with a
glass of wine and a few cicchetti at the counter.
