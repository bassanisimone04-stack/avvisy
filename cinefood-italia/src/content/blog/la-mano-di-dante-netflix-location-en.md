*In the Hand of Dante*, directed by **Julian Schnabel** and produced by
**Martin Scorsese**, has one of the starriest casts in recent memory:
Oscar Isaac, Gal Gadot, Gerard Butler, Jason Momoa, John Malkovich and Al
Pacino. The film alternates a New York smuggling story with a parallel
tale of Dante Alighieri fleeing to Sicily — and much of it was genuinely
filmed across **Veneto** and **Sicily**.

## Venice

Venetian locations include the **Marciana Library** in St Mark's Square,
the island of **Forte di Sant'Andrea**, **Palazzo Rocca Contarini Corfù**
and **The Gritti Palace**, the historic hotel on the Grand Canal. The
Gritti is also home to **Bar Longhi**, one of Venice's most elegant
historic bars, open to the public — a good stop if you're following the
film's trail (it isn't confirmed whether the bar itself appears on
screen).

## Verona and Padua

In **Verona**, the crew used the State Archives and the Tower of the
Castelvecchio Museum; in **Padua**, the CNR (National Research Council)
building.

## Palermo

For the scenes set in Dante's Sicily, filming took place in **Palermo's
historic center**, the port area and the Partanna neighborhood.

## Medieval Lazio

Some medieval-set scenes were filmed around **Tarquinia**, **Bracciano**
and **San Pellegrino**, the medieval quarter of Viterbo.

## What to eat along the way

- In **Venice**, **cicchetti** and a spritz at the bar, the classic
  Venetian way;
- In **Verona**, Valpolicella wines (Amarone above all) and risotto
  all'Amarone;
- In **Palermo**, great Sicilian street food: arancine, pane e panelle,
  sfincione.

## In short

*In the Hand of Dante* is a rare case of a Hollywood production that
crossed half of Italy, from Venice to Sicily. If you're into recent
Sicilian film locations, also read our piece on
*[Nolan's The Odyssey in Favignana](/en/blog/the-odyssey-nolan-sicilia-favignana)*
