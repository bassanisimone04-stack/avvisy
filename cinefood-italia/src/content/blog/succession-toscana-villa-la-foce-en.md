The famous Italian arc of *Succession* — HBO's Emmy-winning series about
the Roy family — wasn't shot on a built set: the estates hosting
Caroline's wedding, in the season 3 finale, are real historic villas in
the heart of **Tuscany**.

## Villa La Foce, the real home of the Roys

The Roy family stays at **Villa La Foce**, a 15th-century estate in the
**Val d'Orcia**, between Florence and Rome. The property, and its now
world-famous gardens, were redesigned in the 1920s by writer **Iris
Origo** together with landscape architect **Cecil Pinsent** — a story as
real as the cypress-lined hills surrounding it.

## Villa Cetinale, the wedding

The season finale's wedding, instead, is filmed at **Villa Cetinale**, a
17th-century baroque residence near Siena. Together with nearby
**Montepulciano**, the two estates make up the show's Tuscan itinerary —
the same territory of **pici pasta**, **Vino Nobile** and **pecorino
cheese** told in our article on
**[Under the Tuscan Sun in Cortona](/en/blog/under-the-tuscan-sun-toscana-cortona)**.

## Dopolavoro La Foce, the real restaurant

Unlike many productions, here you can actually eat in the same place:
**Dopolavoro La Foce**, the estate's restaurant, is open to the public at
Strada della Vittoria 90, with a kitchen built around ingredients from
the surrounding gardens and hills.

## In short

*Succession* confirms how beloved the Val d'Orcia is to international
film and TV: Villa La Foce and Villa Cetinale remain there, open to
visitors, among the most photographed hills in Tuscany. Also read our
itinerary on **[Lake Como on
screen](/en/blog/lago-di-como-nel-cinema-dove-mangiare)**, another
setting from the show.
