If there is one film made for lovers of cinema and food, it is *The Trip to
Italy* (2014), directed by **Michael Winterbottom**: **Steve Coogan** and
**Rob Brydon** cross Italy eating in the most beautiful restaurants,
between jokes and breathtaking landscapes. The film's structure is
entirely built around food: six meals at six real restaurants, over six
days, which Coogan's character is fictionally reviewing for the Observer
Magazine — a real food itinerary to retrace stop by stop.

## The journey's stops

The film follows the boot from north to south. Among the real restaurants
named by the sources: Trattoria della Posta in **Monforte d'Alba** (Piedmont),
La Cantina in **San Fruttuoso** (Liguria), Trattoria Albana in **Mazzolla**
(Tuscany), Oliver Glowig in **Rome**, Relais Blu on the **Sorrento** coast and
Il Riccio on **Capri**:

- **Piedmont**, with truffles and the Langhe;
- **Liguria**, with pesto and the sea;
- **Tuscany**, among hills and wine;
- **Rome**, with its authentic cuisine;
- The **Sorrento coast** and **Capri**, among lemons and fish.

## What to taste along the way

Every region has its flagship dish: **trofie al pesto** in Liguria, **pici** and
steak in Tuscany, **cacio e pepe** in Rome, **scialatielli with seafood** on the
Amalfi Coast. An itinerary that is a true education in Italian taste.

## A film that works as a travel guide

*The Trip to Italy* works as a ready-made itinerary: just follow it to build a
food tour from the top to the bottom of the boot. For our suggestions on where to
eat like in the movies, browse the **[venues](/en/locali)** and the
**[films](/en/film)** on the site.

## In short

*The Trip to Italy* is proof that the best way to visit Italy is… at the table. A
map-film for food lovers. Safe travels and buon appetito!
