*Rocco Schiavone* rests on a contrast: a Roman deputy police chief,
gruff and allergic to rules, exiled to the mountains of the Aosta Valley
as a punishment. Marco Giallini plays the character from **Antonio
Manzini**'s novels, and the Rai series really did film in Aosta, with the
support of the Fondazione Film Commission Vallée d'Aoste and the regional
government.

## The Aosta locations

For season 4, the regional film commission lists among the city sets
**Via Porta Praetoria**, the **Roman Theatre**, the **Pépinière
d'Entreprises** — used as the police headquarters — the **Beauregard
Hospital**, the Maria Adelaide school and the historic **Caffè Nazionale**
on Piazza Chanoux.

## A café reopened for the set

The story of **[Caffè Nazionale](/en/locale/caffe-nazionale-aosta)** is
the most curious of the lot. The venue, a nineteenth-century historic
café under the town hall arcades, was **closed** at the time of filming:
it was reopened **specifically for the set**, under an agreement between
the production and the City of Aosta.

The closure was not a temporary one. The Nazionale sat empty for around
two years before genuinely reopening to the public on **27 August 2022**
under chef **Paolo Griffa**, as "Paolo Griffa al Caffè Nazionale". Today
it brings together a restaurant, patisserie, salon de thé and cocktail
bar under one roof, and has entered the Michelin Guide. The historic
decagonal room, known as "la Chapelle", has been preserved.

## What to see in Aosta

Aosta is a Roman city in miniature: the **Arch of Augustus**, the **Porta
Praetoria**, the **Roman Theatre** and the cryptoporticus are all within
a ten-minute walk. Piazza Chanoux, with its arcades, is the exact centre
of town — and the place to stop.

## In short

Few film locations can claim to have been reopened for a series and then
genuinely brought back to life. Caffè Nazionale is one of them: the show
used it while it was closed; today you can actually walk in. Discover
more **[films and shows](/en/film)** shot in Italy.
