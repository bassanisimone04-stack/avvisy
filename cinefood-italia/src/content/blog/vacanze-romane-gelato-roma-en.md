**Roman Holiday filming locations** read like a map of the Rome everyone dreams of.
In 1953 William Wyler followed Audrey Hepburn and Gregory Peck around the city, from
St Peter's Square to the Spanish Steps, the Mouth of Truth and a river party beside
Castel Sant'Angelo. Most of those places are still there. This guide lists the real
filming locations in Rome, tells you which ones you can actually enter, and says where
to stop for a coffee or a gelato.

## Why these Roman Holiday filming locations still matter

*Roman Holiday* won Audrey Hepburn the Oscar for Best Actress, and it did something
unusual for an American film of its time: it left the studio and went out into the real
city. That is why the locations are so recognisable today. A day is enough to see
most of them, if you start early and wear comfortable shoes, because the route mixes
stairways, bridges and long walks.

## Roman Holiday filming locations in Rome: the route at a glance

According to **Italy for Movies**, the Ministry of Culture's portal, the film goes through
these places:

- **St Peter's Square** and **Via dei Fori Imperiali**: the princess's arrival and the
  parade.
- **Palazzo Barberini** and **Via delle Quattro Fontane**: the embassy and the escape.
  Palazzo Barberini today houses the National Gallery of Ancient Art, so you can visit it.
- **Piazza della Repubblica**, at the Naiadi fountain: where she gets out of the car.
- **Via Margutta 51**: Joe Bradley's apartment.
- **The Spanish Steps** and **Trinità dei Monti**: the gelato scene.
- **The Colosseum** and **Piazza Venezia**: stops on the Vespa tour.
- **The Mouth of Truth**, at Santa Maria in Cosmedin.
- **Ponte Sant'Angelo** and **Castel Sant'Angelo**: the party on the river.
- **Palazzo Colonna**: the final press conference.

## Where was the Roman Holiday gelato scene filmed?

The most famous gelato in film history was eaten on the **Spanish Steps**, in front of
the church of Trinità dei Monti. Today the staircase is a place to walk, not to sit:
Rome has had rules against sitting on the steps since 2019, so enjoy the gelato standing
nearby and check the current rules. One blog names a specific gelato shop as the film's
spot, but we found no solid source, so we don't list it.

## The Mouth of Truth: the improvised scene

One of the best-loved sequences takes place at the **Mouth of Truth**, in the portico of
the church of Santa Maria in Cosmedin. Gregory Peck puts his hand into the stone mouth
and pretends it has been bitten off. It **wasn't in the script**: it was the actor's own
improvisation to scare Audrey Hepburn, who hadn't been warned, so her startled reaction
is genuine. Sources differ on the exact spot of the shoot, the portico or the courtyard.

## Via Margutta 51: Joe Bradley's apartment

The journalist's apartment is at **Via Margutta 51**, on a quiet street long known for
its artists' studios. It is a **private residence**, so you can see the street and the
entrance, not the interiors. According to some guides the interiors were shot on a
soundstage while the exterior and the courtyard were real.

## Palazzo Colonna and Castel Sant'Angelo

The film ends with the princess's press conference in the **Great Hall of the Galleria at
Palazzo Colonna**, in Piazza Santi Apostoli. The palace opens to visitors only on limited
days, so check its official website before planning the stop. Earlier, the party on a
river barge was shot beneath **Ponte Sant'Angelo**, with the castle behind: today you can
walk the bridge, lined with Bernini's angels, for free.

## Roman Holiday locations in Rome: a route on foot or by Vespa

A logical order, from the centre outwards:

1. **Via Margutta** and the **Spanish Steps**, in the same area.
2. **Piazza Venezia** and the **Colosseum**.
3. **The Mouth of Truth**, a short walk from Piazza Venezia.
4. **Palazzo Colonna**, if it is open.
5. **Ponte Sant'Angelo** and **Castel Sant'Angelo**.
6. **St Peter's Square**, to end.

## Where to eat on the Roman Holiday route

The honest answer is that no open restaurant is documented as a set of the film. The
**Rocca café** (Via della Rotonda 25, by the Pantheon), where Joe, Ann and the
photographer drink champagne and cold coffee, is described by guides as gone, with a
clothing shop in its place; one travel site thinks it may even have been invented for
the film. So we don't list it as a venue.

Near the route, the area around the Pantheon is full of historic coffee bars such as
**Tazza d'Oro** and **Sant'Eustachio**, with no link to the film. For restaurants and
cafés that really appear in films, see our **[Rome page](/en/roma)** and the article
**[Rome on Screen: 5 Film Restaurants](/en/blog/roma-nel-cinema-ristoranti-da-film)**.
