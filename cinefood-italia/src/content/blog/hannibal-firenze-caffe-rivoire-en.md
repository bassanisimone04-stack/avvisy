In 2001 Ridley Scott took Hannibal Lecter out of the United States and
hid him in the heart of Florence. The result is a film that, plot aside,
also works as one long tracking shot across the city: the Florentine
shoot ran through spring and summer 2000, across dozens of locations in
the monumental centre.

## The Florence locations

Recognisable places include **Piazza della Signoria**, **Palazzo
Vecchio**, the **Basilica of Santa Croce** and the **Spedale degli
Innocenti**. The narrative anchor is Palazzo Capponi, where Dr Lecter
works under the false name "Dr Fell" as library curator — the post that
puts him in the path of inspector Rinaldo Pazzi, played by Giancarlo
Giannini.

## Caffè Rivoire

Facing Piazza della Signoria, among the city landmarks recognisable in
the film is **[Caffè Rivoire](/en/locale/rivoire-firenze)**, at number
5r. It's one of Italy's best-known historic cafés: opened in **1872** by
Enrico Rivoire, chocolatier to the House of Savoy, it began life as a
chocolate house — and its thick hot chocolate is still the most requested
item on the menu. The tables under the arcades look straight out at
Palazzo Vecchio and the Fountain of Neptune.

## Planning your visit

Piazza della Signoria is the natural starting point: the Uffizi, Ponte
Vecchio and Santa Croce are all a few minutes' walk away. Rivoire is a
historic café with the table-service prices of one of Europe's most
photographed squares: standing at the bar costs considerably less.

## In short

*Hannibal* remains one of the films that used Florence best as a set, and
Rivoire is the easiest way to step inside it: a table, a hot chocolate
and the same view the film carried around the world. Discover more
**[films and shows](/en/film)** shot in Italy on our site.
