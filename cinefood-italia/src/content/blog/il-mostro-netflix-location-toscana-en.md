*The Monster of Florence* (*Il Mostro*, 2025), the Netflix miniseries
directed by **Stefano Sollima**, brought renewed attention to one of the
most discussed true crime cases in Italian history. Beyond the plot, the
series is also a journey through the **real Tuscany**: most of it was
filmed in and around Florence, in places you can actually visit.

## Florence, historic center and outskirts

Many scenes were filmed in the heart of Florence, with streets and squares
dressed to recreate the 1960s and '70s: **Piazza San Firenze**, **Via
Ghibellina**, **Viale della Giovine Italia**, **Via del Proconsolo** and
**Via dell'Anguillara**. The former Court of Appeal in Piazza San Firenze
was used for the trial scenes.

## Montalcino and Sant'Angelo in Colle

Filming also moved to the **Siena** area, with stops in **Montalcino** and
the nearby hamlet of **Sant'Angelo in Colle** — the heart of Brunello wine
country, one of Italy's most celebrated red wines.

## Pisa, Siena and the Florentine Chianti

Other Tuscan locations include **Lajatico** and **Terricciola** (province
of Pisa), the historic center of **Siena**, and several towns between
Chianti and the Florence outskirts, including San Casciano in Val di Pesa,
Mercatale Val di Pesa and Signa.

## What (and where) to eat in this Tuscany

The hills around these locations are among Italy's most famous food and
wine regions:

- In **Montalcino**, **Brunello DOCG**, one of Italy's great red wines;
- In **Siena**, **panforte** and **pici**, the local hand-rolled pasta;
- In the **Florentine Chianti**, bistecca alla fiorentina and local
  extra-virgin olive oil.

*Note: these are recommendations about the area's typical cuisine, not
specific venues that appeared in the series — no bar or restaurant has
been confirmed as an actual filming location so far.*
