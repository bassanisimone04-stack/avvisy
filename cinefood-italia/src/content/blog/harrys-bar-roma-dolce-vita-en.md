While the Café de Paris shut its doors in 2008, a little further along
the same Via Veneto, another historic hangout of Rome's "dolce vita" is
still open: **Harry's Bar**, at the same address since 1918.

## A century of history on Via Veneto

Born as "Golden Gate" in 1918, the venue took its current name in the
1960s, becoming the American Bar and restaurant it still is today. On
its own official website, Harry's Bar dedicates a page to its history
tied precisely to the years of Fellini's "dolce vita": in the 1950s and
'60s it was one of the hangouts where paparazzi photographed
international stars — the same high-society phenomenon Fellini
portrayed in his 1960 film (though he recreated Via Veneto on a
soundstage at Cinecittà).

## Still open today

Unlike many historic venues on the street, **[Harry's
Bar](/en/locale/harrys-bar-roma)** never closed: it's open every day,
with a long menu of classic cocktails and Italian cuisine, on the very
stretch of sidewalk that was the beating heart of high-society Rome in
the 1960s.

## In short

Anyone looking for the real atmosphere of Rome's dolce vita can still
sit at the same bar an entire era once did. Discover also
**[the Café de Paris](/en/blog/la-dolce-vita-via-veneto-cafe-de-paris)**,
the other symbol — now closed — of the same Via Veneto, or the full list
of **[movie restaurants](/en/blog/ristoranti-veri-nei-film-italia-lista)**.
