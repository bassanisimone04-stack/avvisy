No film has portrayed high-society Rome like Federico Fellini's *La Dolce Vita*
(1960). The heart of that world of paparazzi, film stars and jet set was
**Via Veneto**, and one of its symbols was the
**[Café de Paris](/en/locale/cafe-de-paris-roma)**.

## The Café de Paris and the film's Via Veneto

Opened in 1948, the Café de Paris became the emblem of the *dolce vita*: outdoor
tables, stars and lurking photographers. Fellini immortalised this atmosphere in
the film, turning Via Veneto into the stage of glamorous 1960s Rome.

> Note: the Café de Paris **closed in 2008**. It remains a piece of Italian
> cultural history, and Via Veneto still preserves the charm of that era.

## What to see today along Via Veneto

Strolling along Via Vittorio Veneto means walking through the film's set: the
grand historic hotels, the cafés, the steep climb up to Porta Pinciana and the
gardens of Villa Borghese. A perfect itinerary for lovers of auteur cinema.

You will find the venue page **[Café de Paris](/en/locale/cafe-de-paris-roma)** and
the film's stops on **[La Dolce Vita](/en/film/la-dolce-vita)**, along with all the
venues of **[Rome](/en/roma)**.

## In short

*La Dolce Vita* is Via Veneto, and Via Veneto is still there: even though the
**[Café de Paris](/en/locale/cafe-de-paris-roma)** has closed, walking along
Fellini's street remains a small tribute to the most glamorous Rome in film
history.
