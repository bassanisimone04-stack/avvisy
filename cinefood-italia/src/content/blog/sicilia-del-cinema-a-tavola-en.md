Sicily is a land of cinema and flavours. Among baroque villages and crystalline
sea, many places on the island have become unforgettable sets — and perfect
occasions to eat like in the movies.

## The Godfather, between Savoca and Forza d'Agrò

The "Sicilian" scenes of *[The Godfather](/en/film/il-padrino)* were shot in
the villages of the Messina area. At **[Bar Vitelli](/en/locale/bar-vitelli-savoca)**
in Savoca, inside the 18th-century Palazzo Trimarchi, Michael Corleone asks
for Apollonia's hand: the venue — which took its name from the film itself —
is still almost identical to 1971.

## Montalbano's cuisine, in the Ragusa area

The series *[Inspector Montalbano](/en/film/il-commissario-montalbano)* made
Punta Secca, Scicli and Ragusa Ibla famous. The inspector is also a gourmet:
on the seafront of **Punta Secca** you can enjoy the seafood dishes he loves
so much, at the historic **[Enzo a Mare](/en/locale/enzo-a-mare-punta-secca)**.

## The magic of Cinema Paradiso

In **Palazzo Adriano**, the beautiful Piazza Umberto I is the heart of
*[Cinema Paradiso](/en/film/nuovo-cinema-paradiso)*, Tornatore's 1990 Oscar
winner: a village where time seems to have stopped, now home to a museum
dedicated to the film.

## Sicily in more recent cinema

The western coast is back in the spotlight thanks to **The Odyssey** by
Christopher Nolan, filmed between Favignana and the Aegadian Islands, and
the Sicilian scenes of **In the Hand of Dante**, filmed in Palermo. Proof
that Sicily still fascinates Hollywood as much as it did in the 1970s.
