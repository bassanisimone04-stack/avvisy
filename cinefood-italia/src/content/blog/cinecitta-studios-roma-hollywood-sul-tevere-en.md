**Cinecittà Studios** in Rome is the «Hollywood on the Tiber»: the largest and oldest
film studios in Europe (Romewise), where *Ben-Hur*, *Cleopatra* and the series *Rome*
were made. If you're searching **Cinecittà: where they filmed** and **how to visit it
today**, here are the confirmed films, the practical information to double-check and
what's still missing (a venue near the entrance to recommend).

## The history of Cinecittà, Hollywood on the Tiber

Cinecittà opened on **28 April 1937**, in the Fascist era, to relaunch Italian cinema
and compete with Hollywood; according to sources the complex had 73 buildings and 21
sound stages. During the war activity moved north and the studios were occupied by the
Germans. After 1950, American producers chose Rome for low costs and locations: the
nickname Hollywood on the Tiber was born.

## The epics: Quo Vadis, Ben-Hur, Cleopatra

- ***Quo Vadis*** (1951): among the first big American historical epics shot in Rome.
- ***Ben-Hur*** (1959): shot here, as several sources report.
- ***Cleopatra*** (1963): the production moved from London to Cinecittà after budget,
  weather and health problems.
- ***Roman Holiday*** (1953): used both the studios and Rome's locations. Read
  *[Roman Holiday Filming Locations in Rome](/en/blog/vacanze-romane-gelato-roma)*.

## Auteur cinema made here

Cinecittà's name is tied to **Federico Fellini**. On the exhibition route you see the head
of Venusia from *Casanova* (1976), and a costume from *The Taming of the Shrew* by **Franco
Zeffirelli** (Romewise). Other titles guides cite (*Once Upon a Time in America*,
the 1968 *Romeo and Juliet*) we couldn't confirm at Cinecittà, so we don't list them.

## Recent HBO and American productions

Martin Scorsese's *Gangs of New York* (2002) rebuilt 19th-century New York here. For the HBO
series ***Rome*** (2005–2007) a Forum was built that, according to Romewise, is still used by
productions. For ***The Young Pope*** (2016) the **Sistine Chapel** was rebuilt to scale,
because you can't film inside the Vatican; the set was created for *Habemus Papam* and
reused for *The New Pope*.

## How to visit Cinecittà today

- **What to see:** the permanent exhibition *Cinecittà si Mostra* (four pavilions) and
  the sets; there is also the **MIAC** museum, with a separate or combined ticket

- **Hours:** for the 2026 edition a listing shows 30 September to 26 October, Wednesday
  to Monday, 10 am–6 pm, last entry 4:30 pm. Other sources say open every day except
  Tuesday: **check the official site**.
- **Guided tour:** in Italian at 10:30, 12:30, 14:30 and 16:30; in English on Saturday at
  11. Book online, especially at weekends.

- **Closed sets:** some sets are closed when a production is under way; check before you go.
