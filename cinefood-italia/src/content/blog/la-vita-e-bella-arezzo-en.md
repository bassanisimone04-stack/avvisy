**Where was Life Is Beautiful filmed?** Roberto Benigni's film (1997, winner of three
Oscars in 1999) was shot mostly **in Arezzo**, in Tuscany. If you search «Life Is
Beautiful filmed in Matera», the answer is no: there is no trace of Matera in the
sources we consulted. Here are the real locations, where they are and where to eat
(with due caution about the venues' status).

## The locations at a glance

According to Italy for Movies the shoot ran from **November 1996 to April 1997**,
mostly in Tuscany and partly in Lazio and Umbria.

- **Arezzo**: Piazza Grande, Via Borgunto, Logge Vasari.
- **Montevarchi**: Villa Masini, Dora's house and the Grand Hotel.
- **Castiglion Fiorentino**: the scene in which Guido is mistaken for King Victor
  Emmanuel III.
- **Cortona**: the Teatro Signorelli, where Guido meets Dora.
- **Papigno (Terni)**: the concentration camp, rebuilt.
- **Ronciglione (Viterbo)**: the station of the deportation train.

## Arezzo: the square of Life Is Beautiful

Arezzo's **Piazza Grande** is the film's square: many scenes were shot here, including
the one with the family on the bicycle, and the love scenes between Guido and Dora,
according to Visit Tuscany. From there the bike rides go down to **Via Borgunto**, where
Guido's bookshop is. The **Logge Vasari** are indicated for some of the night «miracle»
scenes. Arezzo is easy on foot: the square, the Logge and Via Borgunto are in the same
old town.

## Montevarchi, Castiglion Fiorentino and Cortona

- **Villa Masini, Montevarchi**: a liberty and Art Deco building, used both as Dora's
  house and as the Grand Hotel where Guido works. Visit Tuscany reports summer openings
  with events and guided tours: (to be verified: current dates and hours).
- **Castiglion Fiorentino**: Benigni's hometown; the scene where Guido is mistaken for the
  king was shot here.
- **Cortona, Teatro Signorelli**: at its entrance Guido meets his «princess», according to
  one source. I found no information on visits and hours.

## The second half: Papigno and the station

The concentration camp is not in Tuscany: it was rebuilt at **Papigno**, near Terni,
in a disused chemical plant turned into film studios (now Umbria Studios, according to
Fanpage). The train leaves instead from **Ronciglione station**, according to one source:
a working station, a stop for film fans rather than a tourist destination.

## Where to eat in Arezzo, and what we do NOT promise

Unlike other films on the site, here we have no venue with confirmed opening and a link to
the shoot. Three historic addresses near Piazza Grande, to verify:

- **Buca di San Francesco** (1929, next to the Basilica of San Francesco): a historic
  restaurant closed during the pandemic and then bought by Patrizio Bertelli, Prada's
  chairman. One source speaks of a restoration; (to be verified: reopening and hours).
- **Caffè dei Costanti**: Benigni filmed in front of this historic café, but according to
  Dissapore it had been closed since November 2021 and was seeking a new operator. Current
  status (to be verified); we don't consider it open.
- **Le chiavi d'oro**, 7 Piazza San Francesco: a restaurant Dissapore reviewed as an
  exception among tourist squares; the review is dated, check whether it is open.
